## Task: the sub-tasks

The agent gave parts of the work to sub-agents and helper streams, and some of them gave parts of theirs to others; each is a sub-task. Judge each sub-task as a small target of its own. The targets are listed below, with how many sub-tasks each has, and then the sub-tasks this call judges.

List the sub-tasks with `subtasks`: those some talks started, or those under one sub-task, one level at a time. Each row gives what the sub-task was asked, what it reported back, what it cost with the sub-tasks under it, its failed steps, the step that started it, and the talks that hold its own work. A row with no parent is a stream nothing in the record can be shown to have started; judge it on its own. Read a sub-task's own work with `talk` on its talks when a decision needs it.

For each sub-task, decide:

- `outcome`: did it do what it was asked? Take the first that fits: `achieved` when its own steps show all of it done; `partly` when they show some of it done; `not_achieved` when they show it failed or stopped before a result; `cannot_verify` when they show neither.
- `report`: is what it reported back true to its own work? `accurate` when its own steps support the report; `inaccurate` when they contradict a material part of it, such as a test said to pass that failed, or a file said to be changed that was not; `unverifiable` when its own steps show it neither way. Check the report against the sub-task's steps, never against the report itself.
- `use`: did the agent that asked use the result? `used` when a later step of that agent acts on it or passes it on; `misled` when it acted on a report you found `inaccurate`, so the work that followed rests on what the sub-task got wrong; `unused` when that agent went on without it; `unknown` when you cannot tell. Cite that later step.
- `dispatch`: was giving this work away reasonable when it was given? `reasonable`; `redundant` when the same work was already done, or already under way in another sub-task, as the record showed at that moment; `misdirected` when the task itself was wrong when it was given: too wide or too narrow, against what the person asked, a command or arguments built wrong, or instructions the agent had already replaced; `unknown`. A sub-task started as a check, or beside others to save time, is not redundant because its result went unused. A `misdirected` dispatch cites the step that gave the task, whose input is the task, and the step that shows it wrong: the person's request, a tool result such as an earlier one or one rewriting the task, or the agent giving the task again. The agent saying in a message that the task was wrong is its own account, and shows nothing. Its work is then `misdirected_delegation`: what the sub-task did, however well, is the cost of the agent that gave it.
- `work`: what its own cost was, with the labels of work given above, or `not_reviewed` when you did not read enough of its work to say. A redundant dispatch is `redundant_delegation`. Any label but `not_reviewed` cites a step of its own work. `misled_by_subtask` is not one of them: it labels the asking agent's work, by part, in the efficiency stage; a sub-agent misled by a sub-task of its own shows in that sub-task's `use`.

An outcome and a report rest on a tool step of the sub-task's own work and the result it returned. What the sub-agent says in its own messages is a claim about its work, as its report back is: citing only its messages leaves the outcome `cannot_verify` and the report `unverifiable`.

Judge each sub-task once, by its id. Sub-tasks with the same decisions, such as the agents of one workflow, can share one answer: list their ids together. Each of them is then checked on its own: an outcome or a report holds for a sub-task only when you cite a step of its own work, in its own talks, so cite a step of each. The result of the step that started it is its report back, a claim, not evidence of what it did. Judge a sub-task alone when it differs. A nested sub-task you judge keeps its own decisions; one you do not judge is covered by none, never by its parent's.

Judge with what was known at that moment, not with hindsight. Read where the cost is: the costliest sub-tasks first, and those with failed steps. You may leave cheap sub-tasks unjudged: asz marks them `not_reviewed`, and says how much cost that leaves. Cite the steps your decisions rest on, and put one sentence on why in `note`. An outcome other than `cannot_verify`, a report other than `unverifiable`, a `redundant` dispatch and any `work` other than the first three need a valid citation of a step other than the sub-task's own words, and a `use` other than `unknown` a citation of a later step of the agent that asked, or asz takes them as the value that claims nothing.
