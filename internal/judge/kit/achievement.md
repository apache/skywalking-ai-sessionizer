## Task: whether each target was achieved

The targets are listed below, each with its criteria, each criterion with an id, and the talks that serve it. For each target, decide whether it was achieved as of the head of the conversation.

"This run" lists the talks of the targets you judge, as the tools show them. They are an overview, not evidence: a row says what a talk was about, never what it proved. Before you decide a criterion, open the step that shows it, with `talk` and `step`: a tool result, a recorded change, or the reply that delivered an answer, whose id a talk row gives as `reply_node`. Cite that step. An input shows what the person asked, never that it was done. Then read the last replies, and the steps that prove or disprove the outcome: failed tools, test and build commands and their results, file changes, and what the person said afterwards. A file change shows in two ways. A recorder may have captured it: read it with `changes` and `diff`. A tool result may show it: a diff, a status, a test that ran against it, or a read of the file after the edit. Both are evidence. When the card says no change was recorded, that says only that no recorder ran, never that nothing changed: read the tool results, and say `not_captured` only when nothing in the record shows the file.

A target's sub-tasks are part of its evidence. Find them with `subtasks` and the target's talk ids. What a sub-agent did counts when its own steps show it, such as a test it ran and the result that came back. That a sub-agent reported it did something is a claim, not evidence of it.

For every criterion of the target, by its id, give one state with citations:

- `met`: the record shows it done, all of what it names, by work for this target. Work done for something else, such as a fix the agent needed along the way, meets none of its criteria.
- `not_met`: the record shows it failed, was not done, was done for only part of what it names, or the work stopped before it. Say in `note` what part is missing: links added for 43 of 61 people do not meet "add the links".
- `unknown`: you could not tell, or could not read the evidence.
- `not_captured`: the evidence would be in something the record did not capture.

For each criterion, say in `reason`, in one sentence, why its state holds, and give `observations` for the steps it rests on: the step's id in `node`, which must be a step the criterion cites; an `excerpt` copied from the step word for word, at most 300 bytes, one passage, never parts joined by `...`; and your `interpretation` of it. Give one observation for each passage you rely on, at most a few for a criterion, and copy each step id exactly from the cite. asz reads what the record says of the step, such as a tool that failed, by itself. A tool that ran without error can report a run that failed: say what the output shows, not that the tool ran.

Every criterion of the target appears exactly once. A `met` or `not_met` holds only when what you cite shows it: a tool step that returned a result, or the person's own later input. The input that asked for the target shows what was asked, never that it was done, and a tool call whose result never came shows nothing. The agent's replies, reasoning and model calls show only what it said, with one exception: when an answer is what a criterion asks for, such as the answer to a question or an explanation asked for within a change, the reply shows whether it was delivered, or which requested part is missing. Set `delivered` on every such criterion and cite the reply; the basis `delivered_answer` alone does not mark them. A file, a document or a change is never shown by a reply: cite the step that wrote or read it. A criterion that cites nothing that shows it is taken as `unknown`. asz takes the level from your states: `achieved` when every criterion is `met`; `partly` when some are `met` and some are not; `not_achieved` when none is `met` and at least one is `not_met`; `cannot_verify` otherwise, and when the target has no criterion.

The basis of the outcome:

- `person_accepted`: the person's own later reaction accepts the result. Cite that input. It accepts what the person was shown: a step the person asked for that the record shows was never done stays `not_met`, even when the person approved the rest.
- `observed_result`: a tool result shows it, such as test output, a command's output or a recorded change. Cite that tool step.
- `delivered_answer`: the target was an answer or an explanation, and the reply delivers it. That the reply holds an answer is evidence it was delivered; whether what the answer says is true is a separate claim, checked like any other. Cite the reply.
- `agent_claim_only`: only the agent says so.
- `no_evidence`: nothing shows it either way.

A basis that nothing you cite shows is weakened to `agent_claim_only` or `no_evidence`.

Then check the agent's claims about the outcome in its replies, such as "the tests pass", "the PR is open" or "fixed". For a delivered answer, list each statement it makes about the code, the system or the record that the record can check, such as which path calls which or why a value changed, as a claim of its own, and check it. An answer delivered but wrong is not achieved. Check each against the tool results: `supported`, `contradicted`, `no_evidence` or `not_captured`. A later correction by the person, such as "it still fails", counts against an earlier claim. Never take the agent's words as their own evidence: a claim supported or contradicted only by the agent's words is `no_evidence`. A target with a contradicted claim is not achieved, whatever its criteria say.

Then give the target's `events`, in the order they were recorded. An event is a delivery of the target's result, or its undoing:

- `delivered`: the result, or one part of it, reached the person: the reply that gave the answer, or the step that wrote, committed, published or changed it. A result delivered in parts, such as two files and the reply that reports them, has a delivery for each.
- `undone`: a delivered result was taken back, such as a revert, a deletion, a rollback, or the agent withdrawing its answer. A request to undo it is not its undoing: cite the step that did it. Name in `criteria` the criteria it took back when it took back only part of the result, such as one of two files, and in `undoes` the keys of the deliveries it took back; leave each empty when it took back the whole.
- `delivered_again`: a corrected or redone result was delivered after an earlier one, or after it was undone. A reply that only repeats a result already delivered, unchanged, such as a closing summary, is no event: it would hide what was found wrong with the earlier delivery. Name in `replaces` the keys of the earlier deliveries it corrects when it corrects only some, such as one of two files; leave it empty when it replaces all of them.

`artifact` says what the event concerns: `answer`, `revision` (a commit, a branch or a pull request), `file`, or `remote` (a change made through an API or on another system). Cite the reply for an answer, and the tool step that did it for anything else. Name each event by a key of your own, such as `e1`, and say in `text` what was delivered or undone. A target with no delivery has no events.

Then give the `contradictions`: evidence that a delivered result is wrong. Name the delivery it concerns by its event key in `event`. Set `conclusion` when the evidence shows the delivered conclusion itself wrong, such as the answer or the cause it named; otherwise name in `criteria` the criteria it shows not met. Cite the evidence: a tool result, an error, or the person's later words. The agent's own words are not evidence. When a corrected result was delivered again, a contradiction of the earlier delivery stays with that delivery.

Then give the target's `account`: what was done for it, by what it was for, as the report shows it.

- For a question or an investigation, `answer`: `asked`, the question as the person asked it, citing their input; `checked`, each thing the agent checked or tried, with what it `found` and the `limits` of what that shows, citing the tool step; `ruled_out`, each possibility the evidence excluded, with the check that excluded it in `found` and how far it holds in `limits`; and `answered`, the answer given, citing the reply. Leave `answer` empty for a target that is not a question.
- For changes to files, `files`: the target's file operations, as the `operations` tool lists them for its talks, grouped by what they were for: each group's `area`, such as a package or a directory; its `category`, such as code, tests, documents or configuration; a `summary` of what the group changed; and its `operations` by id. Name each operation once. The report lists the target's operations you leave out as not grouped.
- For a change made through an API or on another system, `remote`: one entry per operation, with the `system`, the `operation`, the tool or API it went `via`, the `request` citing the tool step that sent it, the `response` citing the step that returned it, and what `confirmed` the change later, such as a check or the person's word. A call shows neither that a change was accepted nor that it was completed: leave `confirmed` empty unless something later shows it.

A part you do not give is empty: no text and no citations.

Then give the `rework`: each round in which the agent did work for the target again. A round has an earlier `attempt`, the agent's steps that tried it; a `trigger` that showed it had to be done again, cited in `trigger_cite`; and the `redo`, the agent's steps that did it again. The trigger is `person` (an input of the person, including a correction typed while the agent was still working), `verification` (a test, a build, a CI run or another check that failed), or `tool` (another tool result, such as an error). The trigger can be the attempt's own step, such as a command whose error showed it had to be run again. The `cause` is `agent_error` when the agent's own work was wrong, `requirement_change` when the person changed what they asked, or `unknown` when the record does not show which. Set `finished` when the redo was completed. A round needs an attempt and a redo: a request repeated with no work between is not one.

Then give the `findings`: problems you observed in the target's work, each anchored `at` the step or input where it happened, which must be a step of the target's work. Say the `problem` in one sentence, why it happened in `explanation`, and in `improvement` what would have avoided it, and cite what shows it. A finding proposes: it changes no state and no level. Give none when there is nothing to say.

Give one answer per target, for every target listed. Put what the outcome rests on in `note`, in one or two sentences.
